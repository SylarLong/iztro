Zi Wei Dou Shu: Chart（紫微斗數星盤） ​

Introduction ​

In Zi Wei Dou Shu（紫微斗數）, a chart（星盤）, also called a natal chart（命盤）, is a chart calculated from a person’s birth date and time. It shows the distribution of palaces（宮位） and stars（星曜）. There are three chart layers: the natal chart（本命盤）, decadal chart（大限盤）, and annual chart（流年盤）. To examine fortune over time, these three layers are overlaid and analyzed together. If you are new to Zi Wei Dou Shu, first read Basic concepts to become familiar with the terminology used here.

How to create a chart ​

The inputs needed to generate a chart are simple: the birth date, hour, and gender. Following the star placement rules（安星訣）, these inputs produce a complete chart in forty-nine steps. If you only want to do chart interpretation, however, you do not need to know how to calculate a chart or memorize the placement rules.

Understanding the chart（星盤） ​

The twelve small boxes around the outside are the twelve life-area palaces（十二人事宮）, or twelve palaces（十二宮） for short. The large central box is the central palace（中宮）. It usually contains basic information, but you can place any information there. The red arrow-like diagram in the center marks the trine and opposite palaces（三方四正）. Its tip points to the soul（命宮）; its tail lies in that palace’s opposite position（四正位）; and its two side tips point to the trine positions（三方位）. See The palace system for a detailed explanation.

The twelve surrounding palaces（十二宮） contain different data but share the same layout. We will use one palace as an example to explain its contents.

The image shows the following information:

  • major stars（主星）

    judge（廉貞） and wolf（貪狼） in the upper left corner of the picture are the major stars（主星）, and the label [-3]（陷） beside each one indicates its brightness.

  • auxiliary stars（輔星）

    The ideologue（地空）, fickle（地劫）, and tangled（陀羅） at the upper center of the image are auxiliary stars（輔星）. As with major stars, [-3]（陷） indicates a star’s brightness. Not every star has a brightness label. See Stars in Zi Wei Dou Shu for more on brightness.

  • miscellaneous stars（雜曜）

    gifted（天才）, ageless（天壽）, gourmet（天廚） and sickly（天月） in the upper right corner of the picture are miscellaneous stars（雜曜）.

  • transit stars（流曜）

    artist(Y)（流曲） and tangled(Y)（流陀） in the middle left of the image are transit stars（流曜）.

  • symbolic stars（神煞）

    In the picture, born（長生） government（官府） shown in small characters in the lower left corner and sinister（白虎） insidious（指背） shown in small characters in the lower right corner are called symbolic stars（神煞）, which are divided into:

    • born（長生）, one of the twelve growth-stage stars（長生十二神）
    • government（官府）, one of the twelve Doctor-cycle stars（博士十二神）
    • insidious（指背）, one of the twelve yearly General-front stars（流年將前十二神）
    • sinister（白虎）, one of the twelve yearly Year-front stars（流年歲前十二神）

  • age（小限）

    The numbers 3 15 27 39 51 63 75 87 99 111 at the bottom of the image indicate the age（小限） cycle. They are expressed as nominal age（虛歲）: at ages 3, 15, 27, and so on, the age（小限） cycle occupies this palace.

  • decadal（大限）

    The bold range 33 - 42 at the bottom of the image marks the decadal（大限） cycle. It means that from nominal age 33 years old through 42 years old, the decadal（大限） cycle occupies this palace for ten years.

  • palace name

    The property（田宅） label in the lower left of the image is this palace’s palace name.

  • Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches（干支）

    The gui-hai（癸亥） in the lower right of the image is this palace’s Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches（干支） pair. Its gui（癸） component is the palace stem（宮干）. In discussion, this palace can also be called the hai palace（亥宮）.

  • four transformations（四化）

    The D（忌） after wolf（貪狼） marks one of the four transformations（四化星）, specifically the birth-year D transformation（生年忌）. Red indicates A（化祿）, blue B（化權）, green C（化科）, and black D（化忌）. The small black block before wolf marks self-transformation（自化） and uses the same color scheme. Here it means wolf undergoes a D self-transformation（自化忌）.

How to interpret a chart ​

A chart（星盤） contains so much information that beginners may feel overwhelmed and unsure where to start. This is a common obstacle when learning Zi Wei Dou Shu（紫微斗數）. This section gives you a sequence for interpreting a chart, helping you build familiarity step by step until you can grasp its broad outlines at a glance.

Tips

Each school has its own chart-reading methods and techniques. This site presents research and collected insights on Zi Wei Dou Shu（紫微斗數） without dividing the material by school. The aim is to make it easier to understand and enjoy the subject.

  1. Find the soul palace（命宮）

    Locating the soul（命宮） palace is the first step in reading a chart. It is as basic as knowing where your mouth is before you eat.

  2. Examine the major stars（主星）

    The major stars（主星） in the soul palace（命宮） are the chart owner’s major stars（主星）. When people say “I am emperor（紫微）” or “she is wolf（貪狼）,” they mean the major stars in that palace. An empty palace（空宮） has no major stars, so we use those in the opposite palace（對宮） to interpret it. If these terms are unfamiliar, see the terminology explanations. Once you have identified the major stars, their traits provide a starting point for analyzing the chart owner’s personality. See The fourteen major stars for their individual characteristics.

  3. Examine the trine and opposite palaces（三方四正）

    For the soul（命宮） palace, the other palaces in its trine and opposite palaces（三方四正） group are the surface（遷移）, career（官祿）, and wealth（財帛） palaces. Their importance to a person’s life should be readily apparent, which is why traditional Zi Wei Dou Shu（紫微斗數） interpretation gives this group considerable weight. Yet a chart also needs balance: what limits your potential is often the weakest point, rather than a single exceptionally strong palace. See The palace system for the meaning and role of each palace.

  4. Examine the spirit palace（福德宮）

    The spirit palace（福德宮） reflects a person’s psychological aspect, including preferences and thinking patterns. Considering both the trine and opposite palaces（三方四正） and the spirit（福德） palace lets you outline their general personality and behavior.

  5. Examine the birth-year four transformations（生年四化）

    The four transformations（四化星） affect the chart owner’s inner feelings. A palace with A（祿） is associated with happiness; one with B（權） prompts a desire for control. A palace with C（科） gives a sense of being respected, so its themes are things the person likes to show. In contrast, D（忌） marks a palace associated with inner conflict or embarrassment: the person may be reluctant to show or discuss that part of life, which can be a source of an inferiority complex or a lack of confidence. These are the chart owner’s subjective feelings; other people may see the same matters differently. See The four transformations for details.

  6. Examine palace-stem transformations（宮干四化）

    Besides the Heavenly Stems of horoscope periods（運限） and the natal chart（本命）, each palace's palace stem（宮干） also produces four transformations（四化）. These transformations reveal how palaces interact and relate to one another. They can help examine questions such as How am I getting along with my partner? and How is my job?.

  7. Analyze the palace of interest（目標宮位）

    The target palace is whichever palace represents a matter of particular interest to the chart owner. Examine the stars（星曜） in that palace and the four transformations（四化） generated by its palace stem（宮干） to assess how that area affects the person.

  8. Examine the time cycles（運限）

    The previous steps analyze the chart owner’s static information. They provide an outline, like a preliminary sketch showing where each part of a painting will go and how large it will be. Time-cycle（運限） analysis then fills in that outline, layer by layer. It describes how the environment affects the person over time, with different cycles covering different scopes and producing different effects. See Time cycles in Zi Wei Dou Shu for details.
